# Melatonin: how to compare it (proposal)

> Written by Claude 2026-11-05. NEEDS RIVER REVIEW. Nothing is built yet.

## The problem
Melatonin is sold from well under 1 mg to 10 mg or more per serving. A price per mg would put the
highest-dose products at the top, which reads as "best value" even though a bigger dose isn't a better
buy for someone who wants a small one. The label amount also isn't always what's in the bottle, so any
independent test of the actual content matters more here than in most categories.

## Options
| Option | How it ranks | Problem |
|---|---|---|
| A. Cost per daily serving, dose shown beside it (**recommended**) | Price ÷ servings, like multivitamins, B-complex and probiotics | Doesn't say which product has "more melatonin", and that's deliberate |
| B. Cost per mg | Price ÷ total mg | Ranks the biggest doses cheapest |
| C. Cost per serving, grouped by dose band (e.g. under 1 mg, 1–3 mg, over 3 mg) | One table, sorted within each band | Band edges would be Claude's choice with no source behind them |

## Recommendation: option A
- Sort by **cost per daily serving** as the label defines it.
- Show beside it, never scored:
  - **melatonin per serving** as labeled (mg), with a filter or sort by dose so people can find the amount they want;
  - **format**: tablet, capsule, gummy, liquid, and **extended or time release** when the label says so;
  - other actives added (e.g. L-theanine, herbs), flagged, since they change what the product is.
- A COA that reports **melatonin potency** on the finished product is recorded like any other COA and
  shows in the tier. No extra score.
- No wording about which dose is right for anyone. The evidence page can quote a source on doses if
  River wants one there.
