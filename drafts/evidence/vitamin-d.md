## Level 1: human meta-analyses and RCTs

### Respiratory infections

_section: What it can and can’t help with · not reviewed · moderate evidence_

Pooled individual data from 25 randomized, placebo-controlled trials (10,933 people) found vitamin D lowered the odds of at least one acute respiratory infection (adjusted odds ratio 0.88). The benefit showed up with daily or weekly doses (0.81) but not with large bolus doses (0.97), and was largest in people who started very low (25-hydroxyvitamin D under 25 nmol/L).

Sources: [1]

### Falls in older adults

_section: What it can and can’t help with · not reviewed · limited or early evidence_

A meta-analysis of 47 randomized trials (58,424 people) found a small drop in falls with vitamin D (relative risk 0.95). The drop was larger when vitamin D was taken with calcium (0.88).

Sources: [2]

### Fractures

_section: What it can and can’t help with · not reviewed · limited or early evidence_

In the same meta-analysis, 24 trials (40,102 people) reported fractures. Vitamin D alone did not lower fracture risk (relative risk 0.95, not significant). Vitamin D with calcium did (0.86, just reaching significance).

Sources: [2]

### Daily or weekly, not large single doses

_section: Doses used in research · not reviewed_

In the respiratory infection analysis, trials gave daily, weekly, or bolus doses (at least 30,000 IU at once, monthly to every three months). Only daily or weekly dosing showed a benefit.
- Daily-equivalent dose groups compared: under 800 IU, 800 to 1,999 IU, 2,000 IU or more

Sources: [1]

### Serious side effects in trials

_section: Safety · not reviewed_

Across the 25 trials in the respiratory infection analysis, vitamin D did not change the share of people who had at least one serious adverse event (adjusted odds ratio 0.98).

Sources: [1]
