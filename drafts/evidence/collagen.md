## Level 1: human meta-analyses and RCTs

### Osteoarthritis pain and function

_section: What it can and can’t help with · not reviewed · moderate evidence_

A 2024 meta-analysis of randomized trials (25 trials and 2,856 patients in its main analysis) found collagen derivatives had small-to-moderate effects on osteoarthritis pain (rated moderate certainty) and function (rated high certainty) compared with control. Its abstract groups all “collagen derivatives” together, which may include undenatured type II collagen, a different product not compared on this site. A 2026 umbrella review of 16 systematic reviews also found favorable results for osteoarthritis.
- Pain: SMD −0.35 (−0.48 to −0.22)
- Function: SMD −0.31 (−0.41 to −0.22)

Sources: [1] [2]

### Skin elasticity and hydration

_section: What it can and can’t help with · not reviewed · limited or early evidence_

A 2026 umbrella review of 16 systematic reviews (113 randomized trials, 7,983 people) reported that collagen was consistently associated with favorable skin outcomes. Grouped as limited until the full paper’s certainty ratings have been read.

Sources: [2]

### Bone and muscle

_section: What it can and can’t help with · not reviewed · limited or early evidence_

The same umbrella review concluded collagen showed benefits for bone and muscle health. The abstract doesn’t give how certain those results are.

Sources: [2]

### Oral health and cardiometabolic outcomes

_section: What it can and can’t help with · not reviewed · limited or early evidence_

The umbrella review describes the results for oral health and cardiometabolic outcomes as mixed.

Sources: [2]

### Side effects in trials

_section: Safety · not reviewed_

The 2024 osteoarthritis meta-analysis found collagen derivatives did not increase withdrawals or adverse events compared with control.
