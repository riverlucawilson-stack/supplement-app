## Level 1: human meta-analyses and RCTs

### Sleep in older adults with insomnia

_section: What it can and can’t help with · not reviewed · limited or early evidence_

A meta-analysis found 3 randomized trials comparing oral magnesium with placebo in 151 older adults. People taking magnesium fell asleep 17.36 minutes faster than with placebo. Total sleep time rose by 16.06 minutes, but that was not statistically significant. The authors rated the trials at moderate-to-high risk of bias, called the evidence low to very low quality, and said it is not good enough for doctors to make well-informed recommendations.

### Elemental magnesium vs compound weight

_section: What magnesium is · not reviewed_

The NIH fact sheet says the forms of magnesium in supplements that the body absorbs more easily are magnesium aspartate, citrate, lactate and chloride. This site compares products by the elemental magnesium on the Supplement Facts panel, not the weight of the whole compound.

Sources: [1]

### Recommended intake

_section: How much magnesium · not reviewed_

The NIH fact sheet lists a Recommended Dietary Allowance of 400–420 mg a day for adult men and 310–320 mg for adult women, from all sources combined.
- Adult men: 400–420 mg/day
- Adult women: 310–320 mg/day

Sources: [1]

### Upper limit for supplements

_section: How much magnesium · not reviewed_

The upper limit for adults is 350 mg a day of magnesium from supplements and medications. It does not include magnesium in food, which the fact sheet says does not need to be limited.

Sources: [1]

### Too much from supplements

_section: Safety · not reviewed_

According to the NIH fact sheet, high intakes from supplements and medications can cause diarrhea, nausea and abdominal cramping, and extremely high intakes can lead to irregular heartbeat and cardiac arrest. In healthy people the kidneys get rid of excess magnesium in the urine.

Sources: [1]
